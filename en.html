Frequently asked questions

Common questions about the export pre-check, trade insurance prep, and the deadlines that follow shipment

What is trade insurance?

Trade insurance protects exporters against risks such as not getting paid on an overseas sale.

Short-term Export Insurance (post-shipment) from the Korea Trade Insurance Corporation (K-SURE) covers the loss when, after goods ship under an export contract with payment terms of two years or less, the exporter can’t collect payment from the buyer, or from the issuing bank in a letter of credit (L/C) transaction.

It covers commercial risks, such as a buyer’s insolvency or payment default, and political risks that can arise in cross-border trade, such as war or restrictions on remittances.

What percentage of a loss does trade insurance cover?

Under K-SURE Short-term Export Insurance (post-shipment), the coverage ratio for standard exports and consignment processing trade is 100% for small and midsize companies, 97.5% for mid-market companies, and 95% for large companies.

The ratio that actually applies can vary with K-SURE’s country underwriting policy and the terms set out in your policy. And a 100% coverage ratio doesn’t mean every unpaid amount is always paid in full. The actual claim payment reflects the amount of the loss, any excluded losses, and amounts received under other insurance or financing arrangements.

To know what’s actually covered on a given deal, check your policy and its terms and conditions.

What documents do I need to file a trade insurance claim?

Short-term Export Insurance calls for different documents and procedures at each stage, from taking out a policy to filing a claim.

When you apply, you go through a credit investigation of the buyer, a request for a coverage limit, and issuance of the policy. After shipment, you have to file an export notice within the required deadline. If the buyer doesn’t pay, you file a notice of loss and then submit your claim along with the supporting documents K-SURE requires.

The exact documents can vary by type of coverage, deal structure and the nature of the loss, so check your policy terms and K-SURE’s guidance on loss notices and claims.

The TROPS Trade Insurance Prep Package organizes the steps and documents you need to apply for trade insurance, deal by deal.

When is the deadline to file a notice of loss under Short-term Export Insurance?

Under K-SURE Short-term Export Insurance (post-shipment), if payment isn’t received, you generally have to notify K-SURE of the loss in writing within one month of the payment due date. If you learn before the due date that something has happened that could lead to a loss, you also need to give notice within the required period.

The export notice deadline after shipment matters too. Under an individual policy, you have to file the export notice within 10 business days of the export date; blanket policies use a different notice method and deadline. If you miss the deadline, as a rule that shipment isn’t covered, unless there is good cause for the delay.

Procedures can vary by product and contract terms, so on an actual deal, check your policy terms and K-SURE’s guidance.

Where can I find export insurance premium subsidy programs?

K-SURE publishes information on trade insurance and guarantee fee subsidy programs run with local governments, related agencies and banks. Some are group policies in which the local government is the policyholder; in others, a company takes out an individual policy and receives a premium subsidy.

Eligible regions, eligible companies, covered insurance types and subsidy amounts vary by program, and a program may close once its budget runs out. It’s best to check K-SURE’s premium subsidy page for programs currently open and their terms.

Does TROPS apply for trade insurance on my behalf?

No. TROPS doesn’t underwrite insurance, review claims or apply for coverage on your behalf, and it doesn’t recommend specific products. Eligibility and terms are decided by the relevant institution, such as K-SURE, under its policy terms and review criteria.

What TROPS does is get you ready to apply. For each deal, we check the eligibility requirements and prepare your deal information in application-ready form, a list of the documents a claim requires, and materials for internal approval.

We also don’t provide legal advice or legal review of contracts, and we don’t guarantee that receivables will be collected.
